Mid-summer is the slowest stretch in Toronto real estate. Listings thin out, families are away, and activity drops before the fall market returns in September.

July 2026 is following that pattern. Here is where things stand heading into August.

## The rate environment

The Bank of Canada held its policy rate steady through the first half of 2026. Borrowing costs for buyers have stabilized. Five-year fixed rates sit below their 2024 peak, and variable-rate mortgage holders are no longer facing quarter-over-quarter increases.

Buyer confidence has improved. Pre-approvals are tracking higher than this time last year, and the number of active buyers in Toronto, Markham, and Richmond Hill has increased through the first half of the year.

## Sales volume and price trends

Sales volume in the Greater Toronto Area is ahead of 2025 on a year-over-year basis but below the pace of 2022. Prices have held steady in the detached and semi-detached segments. The condo segment is flat.

The headline numbers tell part of the story. What matters more is what is happening at the street level.

## Detached and semi-detached homes in the 416

Detached homes under $1.3 million in strong school zones are moving. Multiple-offer situations are still occurring in neighbourhoods with transit access and green space.

Semi-detached homes in Riverdale, Leslieville, and Roncesvalles are active. Sellers pricing at or near recent comparable sales are not sitting long. Properties listed 5% above comparable sales are sitting for 30 days or more.

The pattern is the same as it has been for the past two years: pricing matters more than presentation.

## Markham and Richmond Hill

In the 905, detached homes in Markham and Richmond Hill are seeing steady demand. The Unionville and Bayview/16th Avenue corridors have been active. Buyers in those areas are deliberate, but well-priced properties are not sitting.

Townhouses and semis in Markham continue to attract first-time buyers and upsizers from the 416. The price point works well for buyers pre-approved in the $900K to $1.2M range.

## The condo market

The downtown condo market has not recovered as fast as the freehold segments. Inventory in the sub-$700K range remains elevated. Days on market are longer, and sellers who need to move are adjusting.

For buyers, the selection in the condo market is better than it has been in several years. The risk is buying a unit with deferred maintenance, weak rental demand, or a building with rising fees. Those factors do not appear in the listing. Do your diligence.

## For sellers this summer

July is not the time to price for the spring market. There are fewer active buyers. Price based on comparable sales from the past 45 days, not what a neighbour sold for in April.

A home sitting for more than three weeks without an offer is almost always a pricing issue. Buyers see everything on the market. A fairly priced home sells. An overpriced home becomes a price reduction story.

## For buyers this summer

Summer is a good time to buy if you are ready. There is less competition than April. Sellers who listed in spring and did not sell are often motivated.

Being pre-approved and ready to move makes the difference. Properties priced correctly in Markham and Richmond Hill do not sit long, even in July.
