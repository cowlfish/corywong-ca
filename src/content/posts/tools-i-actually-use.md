I'm skeptical of articles that recommend twelve tools for productivity. Here's a shorter list — the software I actually open every day and haven't replaced in at least two years.

## Obsidian

My notes live in Obsidian. Plain markdown files in a folder, synced across devices. No proprietary format, no lock-in. I've been using it since 2021 and I've never had a note disappear, corrupt, or become inaccessible because the company changed direction.

The graph view is mostly decorative. The daily notes plugin and a consistent folder structure do the actual work.

## Things 3

Task manager. I tried Todoist, I tried Notion, I tried a paper notebook. Things 3 is the one I actually maintain. The design is calm, the capture is fast, and the project structure maps well to how I think about work.

## Raycast

Replaced Alfred on my Mac. Launches apps, runs calculations, does clipboard history, has a built-in window manager. It's free. I don't think about it, which is the whole point.

## Linear

For anything involving more than two people, Linear is the clearest project tracker I've used. We use it internally and it's made a real difference in staying coordinated without extra meetings.

---

The theme here is software that stays out of your way. The best tools are the ones you forget you're using.
