Buying a home for the first time is a significant decision. The process is manageable, but it has more steps than most people expect. Here is what to know before you start.

## Get pre-approved first

Before you look at a single listing, get pre-approved for a mortgage. Pre-approval tells you your borrowing limit and locks in a rate for 90 to 120 days. It also tells sellers and their agents you are ready to move.

Work with your bank or a mortgage broker. Brokers compare rates across multiple lenders and often produce a better rate. Either way, get the pre-approval letter in writing before you book showings.

One important point: pre-approval is not a guarantee of final approval. Final approval happens after you have a firm offer and the lender reviews the specific property. Budget conservatively and leave room.

## Budget beyond the purchase price

The purchase price is not the full cost. First-time buyers in Toronto frequently underestimate what closes in addition to the purchase price.

Land transfer tax is the largest extra cost. In Ontario, all buyers pay provincial land transfer tax. Toronto buyers pay an additional municipal land transfer tax. First-time buyers receive a partial rebate on both, but the net amount is still significant on most purchases.

Legal fees run $1,500 to $2,500 for a standard residential transaction. Your real estate lawyer handles title search, closing documents, and registration.

A home inspection costs $500 to $800. For any resale property, this is worth paying. The inspection report gives you a clear picture of the property's condition before you commit.

If your down payment is below 20%, CMHC mortgage default insurance is mandatory. The premium is added to your mortgage principal.

Plan for 2% to 4% of the purchase price in total closing costs.

## What the search looks like

Toronto's market is not uniform. It varies by neighbourhood, property type, and price range.

Detached homes in the 416 with strong school ratings are holding value and moving quickly in the right price range. Semis and townhouses in Leslieville, Roncesvalles, and Danforth East sell fast when priced at market.

In Markham and Richmond Hill, there is more inventory and more time to review properties. Buyers in those areas have more room in negotiations than they did two years ago.

Condos are a different segment. The sub-$700K downtown condo market has real selection. If a condo is your first step, the options are better than they have been in several years.

Expect to see 10 to 20 properties before finding the right one. Your agent keeps you focused on properties matching your criteria, your budget, and your timeline.

## The offer process

Once you find a home, your agent prepares an offer. Conditional offers are common in Toronto's residential market in 2026. Standard conditions cover financing and inspection.

The seller accepts, rejects, or counters. Conditions have a defined timeline, usually five to ten business days. During that period, you complete the inspection and confirm final financing. If either condition is not met, you withdraw the offer and recover your deposit.

When conditions are waived or met, the deal is firm.

## Closing

Closing happens on the date agreed in your offer. In the days before, your lawyer receives all documentation, you transfer the remaining funds, and the property is registered in your name.

Budget for any immediate repairs identified in the inspection, the first mortgage payment, and utility setup costs.
